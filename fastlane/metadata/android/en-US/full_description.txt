SonicCore is a single place to control every audio device your phone can reach — wired
headphones, USB DACs, Bluetooth earbuds, and network speakers — instead of hunting
through system settings that expose only a fraction of what the Android audio stack can
actually do.

<b>Per-device profiles</b>
Every device gets its own EQ, effects and volume settings, applied automatically when it
connects. Your studio headphones and your car stereo stop fighting over one global EQ.
Devices are identified by a stable key that survives reconnects, so a profile stays
attached to the right headphones.

<b>Equalizer that shows its work</b>
Graphic and fully parametric modes, drawn from a real biquad filter chain (RBJ cookbook
coefficients) rather than a decorative curve. Live frequency response, a spectrum
analyser, and 23 built-in presets. AutoEQ preset files can be imported directly, so
measured corrections for your exact headphone model work out of the box.

<b>Effects and dynamics</b>
Crossfeed for headphone listening, stereo width and balance, channel swap and phase
inversion, bass boost, virtualisation, reverb, a compressor and a limiter that is on by
default so processing cannot clip your output.

<b>Per-app mixer</b>
See which apps hold audio sessions and adjust what the platform actually permits.
SonicCore is explicit about limits: where Android forbids per-app volume control, it
says so instead of showing a slider that does nothing.

<b>Microphone engine</b>
Input source selection, gain, noise gate with separate attack and release, de-esser,
input EQ, and live monitoring with a level meter.

<b>Automation</b>
Rules that react to device connection, time of day, battery level and headset actions,
with priorities and cooldowns so overlapping rules behave predictably.

<b>System integration</b>
Quick Settings tiles, a home screen widget, and a foreground service that keeps profiles
applied while you use other apps.

<b>Honest about the platform</b>
Android restricts a lot of audio control, and manufacturers restrict more. Where a
feature is unavailable on your device, SonicCore explains which permission or API is
missing rather than failing silently. A built-in diagnostic log records what the platform
refused, so bug reports can be specific.

<b>This F-Droid build</b>
Contains no proprietary dependencies and no Google Play Services. Chromecast support is
therefore unavailable in this build — it requires a closed-source Google SDK. AirPlay
streaming is unaffected, because it is implemented from scratch over RTSP. Everything
else is identical to the GitHub release.

No ads, no tracking, no analytics, no network access except for discovering and streaming
to speakers on your own network.
